Three things to know

Dew point tracks moisture

It helps compare how much water vapour is in air at different temperatures. A lower dew point generally means drier air.

Compare like with like

VentiSmart compares forecast outdoor dew point with your chosen indoor target dew point—not outdoor and indoor RH percentages.

Use it as a guide

A favourable forecast is one useful signal. Comfort, heating needs, outdoor pollution and the source of moisture still matter.

Why not just look at relative humidity?

Relative humidity changes with temperature. Cool air can show a high RH while still carrying less water vapour than warmer indoor air.

19°C · 55% RH

Dew point: about 9.7°C

Outdoors · example forecast

15°C · 70% RH

Dew point: about 9.6°C

Even though the outdoor RH is higher in this example, its dew point is slightly lower. That suggests the outdoor air contains slightly less moisture and may help lower indoor moisture when brought inside and warmed. The difference is small, so treat it as a cue—not a guarantee.

A few useful terms

Dew point
The temperature air would need to cool to (at its current moisture content) for water vapour to begin condensing. Higher dew point generally indicates more moisture in the air.
Relative humidity (RH)
How close air is to saturation at its current temperature, expressed as a percentage. RH can change when temperature changes, even if the amount of water vapour stays the same.
Indoor target dew point
The app calculates this from the indoor temperature and target RH you set. It is a comparison threshold, not a measurement of your actual room.
Purge ventilation
A short period of increased ventilation to exchange indoor air with outdoor air. Whether and how to ventilate depends on the building, weather and your situation.

A helpful signal, not the whole picture

VentiSmart uses forecast temperature and humidity to estimate outdoor dew point, then compares it with your calculated indoor target. Forecasts can be inaccurate, and the app does not measure your room, airflow, surface temperatures, pollutants or moisture sources.

Use your judgement: consider comfort and heat loss, and follow relevant local health or building guidance. Ventilation may not address leaks, persistent condensation or other underlying damp problems.
